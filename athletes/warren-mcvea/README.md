# Warren Douglas McVea — A1 permanent life and football record

**Canonical A1 subject:** Warren Douglas “Wondrous Warren” McVea  
**Born:** July 30, 1946 — San Antonio, Texas  
**Died:** October 2025, age 79 — **exact date disputed**: Pro Football Archives and Pro-Football-Reference list October 18, 2025; the Associated Press reported that he died Sunday, October 19, 2025, at home in Los Angeles. The Kansas City Chiefs remembrance also gives an inconsistent death location (San Antonio). Do not silently resolve either dispute.  
**High school:** Brackenridge High School, San Antonio, Texas  
**College:** University of Houston, 1965–1967 letter seasons; archives also list 1964 institutional participation  
**Positions:** Running back, split end, wide receiver; kick returner  
**Height/weight:** Approximately 5 ft 9–10 in, 182 lb; source listings vary  
**Professional jerseys:** Cincinnati Bengals No. 42; Kansas City Chiefs No. 6  
**Primary football era:** 1968–1973 NFL/AFL appearances  
**Championship:** 1969 AFL champion, Super Bowl IV champion with Chiefs (January 11, 1970)

## High school — San Antonio Brackenridge
- Nicknamed “Wondrous Warren.”
- Helped Brackenridge win the Texas state championship in 1962, according to the Associated Press.
- The Texas High School Football Hall of Fame credits him with **591 career high-school points**.
- On **November 29, 1963**, in the UIL Class 4A bi-district playoff at Alamo Stadium, Brackenridge lost to San Antonio Lee **55–48**. The **UIL** credits McVea with **215 rushing yards and six rushing touchdowns** (36 points from touchdowns; the Texas High School Football Hall of Fame reports 38 total points).\n- **1963 senior season:** San Antonio Sports Hall of Fame records **1,332 rushing yards on 127 carries** and **315 points**.\n- **1962 championship season:** San Antonio Express-News retrospective credits McVea with **24 touchdowns despite missing three games** through injury, including **10 postseason TDs**.
- The Hall of Fame records 9.5-second 100-yard speed and describes him as one of Texas's greatest high-school backs of the 1960s.
- Also competed in track and field as a sprinter.
- **Texas High School Football Hall of Fame — Class of 1983.**

## University of Houston
- His enrollment and football scholarship broke a significant racial barrier at a major Texas football program.
- Under head coach **Bill Yeoman**, he became the first Black football player at the University of Houston, with contemporaneous and later sources describing his status as the first Black athlete to receive a football scholarship at a major Texas school.
- Houston Athletics describes three playing seasons, **1965–1967**, totaling **1,461 rushing yards, 841 receiving yards and 17 touchdowns**.
- The Chiefs, the Hall of Fame and other accounts credit **3,009 all-purpose yards**, a school record at the time.
- In 1966 against Washington State, he caught a **99-yard touchdown pass** in the first college football game played on artificial turf.
- Recognized as a 1967 second-team All-American; the Texas high-school hall profile calls him a two-time All-American without clarifying the separate selectors.
- Inducted into **San Antonio Sports Hall of Fame (2003)** and **University of Houston Athletics Hall of Honor (2004)**.

- Bengals fourth-round draft pick, **No. 109 overall in the 1968 draft**.
- Traded from Cincinnati to Kansas City in September 1969.
- Joined a backfield featuring Mike Garrett and Robert Holmes.
- Career NFL/AFL receiving: **38 receptions, 358 yards, 2 TD**.
- Career NFL/AFL kickoff returns: **47 for 1,008 yards**.
- NFL/AFL offensive touchdowns: **13** total (11 rushing, 2 receiving).
- NFL/AFL rushing average: **4.8 yards per attempt** (248 carries).
- Also completed a touchdown pass; the archival statistics list one 50-yard scoring pass in 1969.
- Played on the Chiefs team that defeated the Vikings **23–7 in Super Bowl IV**; Associated Press credits **12 carries for 26 yards** in the game.
- A 1972 injury/reserve year should **not** be counted as a played season.
- Pro Football Archives additionally documents **1974 WFL Houston–Shreveport** involvement, plus other WFL roster transactions, and a 1976 Houston Oilers signing; **distinguish transactions/signings from confirmed regular-season game appearances**. The archive gives 1974 H-S 70 carries, 219 yards, one receiving touchdown. Investigate team naming and 1975/1976 service before expanding career totals.
- Served in the United States Marine Corps; dates and service chronology merit additional documentation.

## Later life and historical context
His football legacy includes pioneering the racial integration of major-college football in Texas. Contemporary reports note that he faced racist threats while at Houston. The Associated Press reported that he served prison time in the 1990s and into 2000 on drug charges; fuller details and context should be checked before publication beyond a careful, sourced life-history record.
