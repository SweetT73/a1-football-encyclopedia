Toya Cardin Jones

October 28, 1976 – October 22, 2026

Refugio High School · Texas A&M · NFL / XFL / CFL · Texas Track & Field Hall of Fame · Sagis Transport

The Life and Career of Toya Cardin Jones

From Refugio to Texas A&M, professional football, and the transportation business he founded, Jones built a career across several fields. His individual achievements are told below in career order, with dated results and original sources beside the events they document. Where game-by-game records remain unavailable, no statistics are invented.

High School Football — Seasons and Honors

At Refugio High School, Jones developed into one of Texas’s most decorated small-school defenders. Contemporary selections trace his rise from a second-team All-State defensive back in 1993 to first-team recognition and a defensive player-of-the-year honor in 1994. Individual game statistics from the earlier seasons still require original game coverage.

Team scores are not treated as proof of Jones’s participation. Track & Field — AAU Youth Results, then High School Seasons

Jones’s track achievements began before high school and grew into an extraordinary run at the UIL state meet. The results below follow his youth competitions and each Refugio season, retaining individual marks, placements, and distinctions between official results and later reports.

1989 — AAU Junior Olympics (before high school)National runner-up, Junior (ages 13–14) boys’ 100m hurdles — 13.99 seconds, second behind fellow Refugio athlete Dana Robinson (13.57, marked as an AAU national record in the original 1989 results). Original 1989 AAU Junior Olympics results, PDF p. 8. Separate AAU record attribution: AAU-affiliated meet results published in 2000, 2003 and 2004 attribute the youth boys’ 100m hurdles national record of 13.34 to Toya Jones of Refugio in 1989. The 13.34 record attribution is repeated by multiple meet records but the original performance/date for 13.34 has not been located; it must not be substituted for the separate, directly documented 13.99 second-place result.
1992 — UIL track (freshman)2 state gold medals: long jump 23 ft 5 in; triple jump 45 ft 11 in, both confirmed in the UIL official 1991–1992 Class 2A state-champions register. Also 8th in 2A 110m hurdles, 16.2 seconds, UIL 1992 hurdles results. The UIL’s 1992 triple-jump URL presently reproduces 1993 data and should not be used to relabel the 1993 49 ft 8¼ in jump as a 1992 result.
1993 — UIL track (sophomore)5 gold medals, 45 individual points: 100m 10.50; 200m 21.40; long jump 24 ft 4¾ in; triple jump 49 ft 8¼ in (2A record); 4×100m 41.3 / UIL record listing 41.44, with Pete Perez, Roy Herron, Dana Robinson and Jones. UIL 1993 triple jump; UIL historical record listings. The year is confirmed by the 1993 archive, notwithstanding a duplicated page under a 1992 URL. The original 100m result lists 10.5, and the original 200m result lists 21.4; the later UIL champions register carries 10.74 and 21.64. These are different reporting conventions, not separate races.
1994 — UIL track (junior)4 gold, 1 silver: 100m 10.2 seconds (original UIL results, new 2A record), with later UIL champions-register listing 10.44 (and an older secondary record-book reproduction listing 10.35); 200m 20.8 seconds (original UIL results, tied 2A record), with later UIL listing 21.04, long jump 25 ft 1 in, triple jump 48 ft 1½ in; 4×100m relay silver 42.2. Golden West Invitational long jump winner 24 ft 8¼ in. Newly recovered 1994 Region IV meet records (separate from UIL state-meet marks): 200m — 21.17 seconds; long jump — 24 ft 8¼ in. These performances are attributed to Jones of Refugio by the original 2001 Region IV-2A meet results (April 27–28, 2001; historical record headings) and are still listed as Region IV records in the April 18–19, 2025 Region IV-3A meet results (2025 official meet results, 200m and long jump sections). The 2025 results confirm the record attribution persisted for 31 years; they do not constitute a new 2025 performance by Jones. The 1994 original regional results sheet remains to be located. Track & Field News ranked Jones No. 1 nationally on its 1994 boys’ long-jump All-America list, above Frank Young and John George; this is a season ranking, not a separate meet victory. T&FN historical All-America rankings, 1994. UIL 100m original; UIL 200m original. International and national junior competitions: 1994 USA Junior Championships 100m runner-up (second place), documented retrospectively in the contemporary The Battalion, July 11, 1995, p. 4; exact final time and meet date still require original results. That finish preceded his selection for the U.S. team at the World Junior Championships in Lisbon. World Junior 100m: heat 10.63 (2nd), quarterfinal 10.58 (3rd), semifinal 10.66 (5th). July 23–24, 1994 World Junior 4×100m relay: ran anchor/fourth leg for the U.S. silver-medal squad (Deworski Odom, Tony Wheeler, Pat Johnson, Jones), which ran 39.95 in its heat and 39.76 in the July 24 final. The relay leg is also described in The Battalion (July 11, 1995); the World Athletics athlete record independently confirms the 39.76 mark and international silver. The international medal and national junior runner-up finish are not UIL State Meet medals.
1995 — UIL track (senior)2 gold, 2 silver, 1 bronze: long jump 25 ft ¼ in (25-00.25) (gold; original 1995 UIL results and UIL record book; corrects the earlier unsupported 25 ft 3¾ in state-meet mark), triple jump 48 ft 8 in (gold), 100m 10.61 (silver), 4×100m 42.14 (silver), 200m 21.63 (bronze). Gatorade Texas Boys Track & Field Player of the Year. Gatorade award record. Additional individual victories at the 68th Texas Relays (April 5–8, 1995): high-school boys’ 100m champion, 10.43 seconds (April 7 final), and high-school boys’ long-jump champion, 21 ft 9½ in. These are Texas Relays titles, not UIL State Meet medals. The original 1995 Texas Relays results also list Jones in the high-school triple jump as ND (no recorded distance); this is an entered-event result, not a third title or a measured jump. Sources: original 1995 Texas Relays results (100m final dated April 7); University of Texas official historical champions list (both events). National competitions: June 10 Golden West fifth in long jump, sixth in 100m; June 16–17, 1995 National Scholastic Outdoor Championships, Raleigh, North Carolina: third in boys’ long jump, 23 ft 10¾ in (+0.6 m/s wind); original meet results published July 6, 1995, National Scholastic results. June 17 Golden South third in long jump; June 23 USA Junior Championships seventh in long jump and withdrew from 100m with hamstring injury. The Battalion, July 11, 1995, p. 4 specifies a left hamstring strain at the May state meet and a right hamstring strain at the USA Junior meet, limiting him to two long-jump attempts. Four-year state-meet career total reported as 147 points by the Houston Chronicle, October 22, 2026; this retrospective total requires reconciliation against UIL scoring and is not assigned to 1995 alone. Track & Field News ranked Jones fifth nationally in its 1995 boys’ long-jump All-America list (T&FN); this is a season ranking, not a meet finish. The Battalion, July 11, 1995, p. 4.
Texas A&M — Football

At Texas A&M, Jones contributed on defense and special teams across four seasons. His senior year culminated in the 1998 Big 12 Championship, where his tackle of Everett Burnett at the one-yard line kept Kansas State from scoring on the final play of regulation.

1995 — Texas A&M footballFour-year college football letter sequence begins. September 2, 1995 vs LSU — confirmed game participation, jersey No. 5, one solo tackle (1-0), no assisted tackles; the official LSU–Texas A&M original game book, September 2, 1995, defensive statistics and participation independently confirms the earlier A1 entry. This is a verified college game appearance and must not be projected backward to his 1991–1994 high-school games.
1996 — Texas A&M football25 tackles (15 solo, 10 assisted); one fumble recovery returned seven yards, as preserved in the earlier A1 biography.
1997 — Texas A&M footballAugust 27, 1997, post-scrimmage depth chart: Texas A&M listed No. 5 Toya Jones OR No. 30 Brandon Jennings at first-string free safety; this establishes a shared/alternative depth-chart designation, not a verified regular-season start. Texas A&M official depth chart, August 27, 1997. 12 games; 30 tackles; two forced fumbles in the 1999 San Francisco 49ers media guide. Earlier research also records one interception; Texas A&M’s February 2, 1998 official banquet report independently confirms his selection as 1997 special-teams captain.
1998 — Texas A&M football13 games, 18 tackles (15 solo, 3 assisted); Special Teams MVP/captain, confirmed by Texas A&M’s January 30, 1999 award-banquet account; its original report also confirms 18 tackles and the Missouri fumble recovery. Sept. 12: helped down a Shane Lechler 69-yard punt at Louisiana Tech 1 (A&M game notes). Nov. 14: recovered muffed Missouri punt to set up winning field goal. Dec. 5: tackled Everett Burnett at 1-yard line at end of regulation of Big 12 Championship; A&M won 36–33 in double OT. Kansas State entered the championship ranked No. 2 with an unbeaten regular-season record and featured Heisman runner-up quarterback Michael Bishop, receiver Aaron Lockett (Tyler Lockett’s uncle), future NFL kicker Martin Gramatica, and future NFL defenders Darren Howard, Mark Simoneau and Ben Leber. Gramatica made four field goals in this game. These opponents provide context for the significance of Jones’s stop; they are not credited as Jones’s individual statistics. Kansas State official media guide; 1998 Kansas State roster.

Texas A&M — Track

Jones also continued competing in track and field for the Aggies. The collegiate results are kept separate from his football statistics so each season can be followed without repeating the same accomplishments.

1995 — Texas A&M trackCollege track letter years are not individually established from the available source; Texas A&M retrospectively identifies Jones as a four-year track letterman. Do not assign the four track letters to 1995–1998 automatically, because Jones was still competing as a senior in May 1999. Texas A&M, January 9, 2020; The Battalion, May 7, 1999, p. 13.
1996 — Texas A&M trackApril 6, 1996, Texas Relays university/college men's 4×200m: sixth place, 1:27.31. Original University of Texas complete meet results identify Jones as the second leg for Texas A&M, with Mike Price first and Billy Fobbs fourth; the third-leg name is blank in the indexed text and is not inferred. This establishes Jones's participation in this specific college meet, separate from his NCAA Outdoor 4×100m qualification. The same original 69th Texas Relays result book (p. 9, Friday April 5 men's university 4×100m preliminary) also names Jones as Texas A&M's second-leg runner, alongside Billy Fobbs and Mike Price; the scanned text interleaves columns, so no heat time or placing is assigned without visual verification. 69th Texas Relays complete results, April 3–6, 1996, p. 33. Qualified for the NCAA Outdoor Championships in the 4×100m relay, the first of three consecutive seasons (1996, 1997, 1998), as confirmed retrospectively by a contemporary May 7, 1999 article in The Battalion, p. 13. Individual relay leg and championship placing in 1996 remain unestablished.

Documented relay wins and marks: College Station Relays 39.95; Baylor/Dr Pepper 39.57; A&M–Texas dual 40.02; Texas Relays 39.04; Penn Relays 38.92; Big 12 39.30; NCAA Outdoor Championships 38.80. 1998 — Texas A&M trackThird consecutive NCAA Outdoor Championships qualification as a 4×100m relay member. Also qualified for the 1998 NCAA Indoor Championships in the 55m dash, confirmed by The Battalion, May 7, 1999, p. 13. Additional marks: February 14 indoor 55m 6.27; February 28 indoor long jump 7.36m, per earlier A1 research citing World Athletics. Qualification does not itself establish an NCAA final placing. May 15–17, 1998 Big 12 Outdoor Championships, Columbia, Missouri — two individual scoring finishes: May 16 long jump: sixth, 7.28m (23 ft 10¾ in), legal wind −0.2 m/s; May 17 men's 100m final: eighth, 10.45 seconds, wind-aided +2.6 m/s (May 16 preliminary heat: fourth in heat, 10.57 seconds, legal wind +1.0 m/s). 1999 — Texas A&M track / 49ersJones was an active Texas A&M senior relay runner in May 1999. Recognition and Business

Jones’s achievements were recognized well beyond his playing years. He later built Sagis Transport, extending his competitive drive into business and creating a legacy beyond athletics.

2003 — Toronto ArgonautsSigned May 1 (earlier A1 research); no Toronto regular-season game established.
May 12, 2010 — UIL All-Century honorsSelected No. 2 in both the boys’ 100-meter and 200-meter dashes on the University Interscholastic League’s fan-voted All-Century Boys Track & Field Teams. UIL listed Jones of Refugio with 10.44 (100m) and 21.04 (200m). The official release says more than 4,700 votes were cast across the project; rankings reflect a fan vote, not a new race or statistical performance. UIL official announcement, May 12, 2010.
2020 — track Hall of FameInducted into Texas Track & Field Hall of Fame January 10, 2020. Texas A&M, January 9, 2020.
2017 / 2023 / 2024 — businessWorked in sales at Sagis Diagnostics in Houston before founding Sagis Transport in Sugar Land on March 6, 2017 (precise founding date in Texas A&M McFerrin Center’s Aggie 100 company profile), per the Houston Chronicle, October 22, 2026. Aggie 100 No. 10 in 2023 (119.90% growth) and No. 20 in 2024 (82.61% growth), independently listed by Texas A&M McFerrin Center. A 2026 memorial biography additionally reports that the company grew to more than 300 employees across 43 states; these operating-scale figures are attributed to the memorial and are not independently verified by a dated company filing.
