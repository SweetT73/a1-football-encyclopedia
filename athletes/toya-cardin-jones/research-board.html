High School Football — Seasons and Honors
1991 — Refugio football (freshman)Earliest requested season. No individual 1991 game appearance, start, tackle, interception or play has been established from the archival material reviewed. Team scores are not treated as proof of Jones’s participation.
1992 — Refugio football (sophomore)No individual game-by-game participation or season totals established in the reviewed primary sources. Do not infer starts or statistics.
1993 — Refugio football (junior)APSE Class 2A Second-Team All-State defensive back, listed as a Refugio junior, 6 ft 2 in, 190 lb. Big Spring Herald, December 16, 1993, p. 9. The roster does not publish Jones’s tackle or interception totals.
1994 — Refugio football (senior)APSE Class 2A First-Team All-State defensive back and Defensive Player of the Year. Big Spring Herald, December 14, 1994, p. 7A; Texas Sports Writers Association historical archive. The earlier A1 biography also records TSWA first-team recognition. Parade’s official 1995 All-America roster identifies Jones as a linebacker, a different position label from the APSE defensive-back selection. A later Texas HS Football retrospective reports a 1994 Texas Super Team selection; original selection list not located.
February 1, 1995 — college football signingTexas A&M national signing day recruit: an Associated Press report by Jaime Aron identifies Jones among A&M's signees, describing him as the Class 2A player of the year and a Parade All-American expected to play defensive back. This confirms recruitment and signing, not an eventual college start. The Pampa News, February 1, 1995, p. 10.
May 12, 1995 — contemporary retrospectiveAn Associated Press feature by Jaime Aaron, preserved in a historical newspaper archive, reported opponents avoided throwing toward Jones’s side. Qualitative observation only, not a statistical interception count. Contemporary AP newspaper reproduction.
July 30, 1995 — football all-star selectionMcDonald’s Texas North–South All-Star Football roster, South team No. 7, wide receiver; 6 ft 3 in, 195 lb, Refugio, Texas A&M. Roster placement verifies selection, not actual game snaps or receptions. Polk County Enterprise, July 30, 1995, p. 11A.
Track & Field — AAU Youth Results, then High School Seasons
1989 — AAU Junior Olympics (before high school)National runner-up, Junior (ages 13–14) boys’ 100m hurdles — 13.99 seconds, second behind fellow Refugio athlete Dana Robinson (13.57, marked as an AAU national record in the original 1989 results). Original 1989 AAU Junior Olympics results, PDF p. 8. Separate AAU record attribution: AAU-affiliated meet results published in 2000, 2003 and 2004 attribute the youth boys’ 100m hurdles national record of 13.34 to Toya Jones of Refugio in 1989. The 13.34 record attribution is repeated by multiple meet records but the original performance/date for 13.34 has not been located; it must not be substituted for the separate, directly documented 13.99 second-place result.
1992 — UIL track (freshman)2 state gold medals: long jump 23 ft 5 in; triple jump 45 ft 11 in (earlier A1 record, original result not rechecked in this pass). Also 8th in 2A 110m hurdles, 16.2 seconds, UIL 1992 hurdles results. The UIL’s 1992 triple-jump URL presently reproduces 1993 data and should not be used to relabel the 1993 49 ft 8¼ in jump as a 1992 result.
1993 — UIL track (sophomore)5 gold medals, 45 individual points: 100m 10.50; 200m 21.40; long jump 24 ft 4¾ in; triple jump 49 ft 8¼ in (2A record); 4×100m 41.3 / UIL record listing 41.44, with Pete Perez, Roy Herron, Dana Robinson and Jones. UIL 1993 triple jump; UIL historical record listings. The year is confirmed by the 1993 archive, notwithstanding a duplicated page under a 1992 URL.
1994 — UIL track (junior)4 gold, 1 silver: 100m 10.35 (earlier A1 result; UIL later lists 10.44), 200m 21.04 (original UIL hand-timed listing 20.8), long jump 25 ft 1 in, triple jump 48 ft 1½ in; 4×100m relay silver 42.2. Golden West Invitational long jump winner 24 ft 8¼ in. Track & Field News ranked Jones No. 1 nationally on its 1994 boys’ long-jump All-America list, above Frank Young and John George; this is a season ranking, not a separate meet victory. T&FN historical All-America rankings, 1994. UIL 100m original; UIL 200m original. International and national junior competitions: 1994 USA Junior Championships 100m runner-up (second place), documented retrospectively in the contemporary The Battalion, July 11, 1995, p. 4; exact final time and meet date still require original results. That finish preceded his selection for the U.S. team at the World Junior Championships in Lisbon. World Junior 100m: heat 10.63 (2nd), quarterfinal 10.58 (3rd), semifinal 10.66 (5th). July 23–24, 1994 World Junior 4×100m relay: ran anchor/fourth leg for the U.S. silver-medal squad (Deworski Odom, Tony Wheeler, Pat Johnson, Jones), which ran 39.95 in its heat and 39.76 in the July 24 final. The relay leg is also described in The Battalion (July 11, 1995); the World Athletics athlete record independently confirms the 39.76 mark and international silver. The international medal and national junior runner-up finish are not UIL State Meet medals.
1995 — UIL track (senior)2 gold, 2 silver, 1 bronze: long jump 25 ft 3¾ in (gold), triple jump 48 ft 8 in (gold), 100m 10.61 (silver), 4×100m 42.14 (silver), 200m 21.63 (bronze). Gatorade Texas Boys Track & Field Player of the Year. Gatorade award record. Additional individual victories at the 68th Texas Relays (April 5–8, 1995): high-school boys’ 100m champion, 10.43 seconds (April 7 final), and high-school boys’ long-jump champion, 21 ft 9½ in. These are Texas Relays titles, not UIL State Meet medals. Sources: original 1995 Texas Relays results (100m final dated April 7); University of Texas official historical champions list (both events). National competitions: June 10 Golden West fifth in long jump, sixth in 100m; June 16–17, 1995 National Scholastic Outdoor Championships, Raleigh, North Carolina: third in boys’ long jump, 23 ft 10¾ in (+0.6 m/s wind); original meet results published July 6, 1995, National Scholastic results. June 17 Golden South third in long jump; June 23 USA Junior Championships seventh in long jump and withdrew from 100m with hamstring injury. The Battalion, July 11, 1995, p. 4 specifies a left hamstring strain at the May state meet and a right hamstring strain at the USA Junior meet, limiting him to two long-jump attempts. Track & Field News ranked Jones fifth nationally in its 1995 boys’ long-jump All-America list (T&FN); this is a season ranking, not a meet finish. The Battalion, July 11, 1995, p. 4.
Texas A&M — Football
1995 — Texas A&M footballFour-year college football letter sequence begins; one documented solo tackle against LSU on September 2, 1995 (earlier A1 record).
1996 — Texas A&M football25 tackles (15 solo, 10 assisted); one fumble recovery returned seven yards, as preserved in the earlier A1 biography.
1997 — Texas A&M footballAugust 27, 1997, post-scrimmage depth chart: Texas A&M listed No. 5 Toya Jones OR No. 30 Brandon Jennings at first-string free safety; this establishes a shared/alternative depth-chart designation, not a verified regular-season start. Texas A&M official depth chart, August 27, 1997. 12 games; 30 tackles; two forced fumbles in the 1999 San Francisco 49ers media guide. Earlier research also records one interception; Texas A&M’s February 2, 1998 official banquet report independently confirms his selection as 1997 special-teams captain.
1998 — Texas A&M football13 games, 18 tackles (15 solo, 3 assisted); Special Teams MVP/captain, confirmed by Texas A&M’s January 30, 1999 award-banquet account; its original report also confirms 18 tackles and the Missouri fumble recovery. Sept. 12: helped down a Shane Lechler 69-yard punt at Louisiana Tech 1 (A&M game notes). Nov. 14: recovered muffed Missouri punt to set up winning field goal. Dec. 5: tackled Everett Burnett at 1-yard line at end of regulation of Big 12 Championship; A&M won 36–33 in double OT.
1995–1998 — Texas A&M football career4 letters; 87 career tackles; 1998 Big 12 champion. Individual season totals above must not be adjusted to force the career total without original game/season records.
Texas A&M — Track
1995 — Texas A&M trackCollege track letter years are not individually established from the available source; Texas A&M retrospectively identifies Jones as a four-year track letterman. Do not assign the four track letters to 1995–1998 automatically, because Jones was still competing as a senior in May 1999. Texas A&M, January 9, 2020; The Battalion, May 7, 1999, p. 13.
1996 — Texas A&M trackApril 6, 1996, Texas Relays university/college men's 4×200m: sixth place, 1:27.31. Original University of Texas complete meet results identify Jones as the second leg for Texas A&M, with Mike Price first and Billy Fobbs fourth; the third-leg name is blank in the indexed text and is not inferred. This establishes Jones's participation in this specific college meet, separate from his NCAA Outdoor 4×100m qualification. 69th Texas Relays complete results, April 3–6, 1996, p. 33. Qualified for the NCAA Outdoor Championships in the 4×100m relay, the first of three consecutive seasons (1996, 1997, 1998), as confirmed retrospectively by a contemporary May 7, 1999 article in The Battalion, p. 13. Individual relay leg and championship placing in 1996 remain unestablished.
1997 — Texas A&M trackNCAA outdoor 4×100m national champion, second leg; team undefeated that season. Documented relay wins and marks: College Station Relays 39.95; Baylor/Dr Pepper 39.57; A&M–Texas dual 40.02; Texas Relays 39.04; Penn Relays 38.92; Big 12 39.30; NCAA Outdoor Championships 38.80. Also the second consecutive NCAA outdoor relay qualification. Texas A&M Hall of Fame account; The Battalion, May 7, 1999.
1998 — Texas A&M trackThird consecutive NCAA Outdoor Championships qualification as a 4×100m relay member. Also qualified for the 1998 NCAA Indoor Championships in the 55m dash, confirmed by The Battalion, May 7, 1999, p. 13. Additional marks: February 14 indoor 55m 6.27; February 28 indoor long jump 7.36m, per earlier A1 research citing World Athletics. Qualification does not itself establish an NCAA final placing.
1999 — Texas A&M track / 49ersJones was an active Texas A&M senior relay runner in May 1999. The Battalion of May 7, 1999, p. 13 identifies the planned 4×100m order as Brandon Evans (first), Toya Jones (second), Billy Fobbs (third), Michael Price (fourth), and reports Jones had already signed with the 49ers, who allowed him to finish his college track season. The report also records Jones's first-person account that he had practiced with San Francisco's second team; this is a reported practice role, not an NFL regular-season appearance. Individual marks: February 20 indoor 60m 6.85; May 8 outdoor 100m 10.38 (+1.1 m/s), second, from earlier A1 World Athletics research. Original Battalion report.

New source distinction (October 25, 2026): The 1994 USA Junior 100m second place is established by a July 11, 1995 contemporary newspaper retrospective, not yet by an original 1994 results sheet. The World Junior relay fourth-leg assignment is likewise supported by the contemporary newspaper and the published lineup order; the official World Athletics record confirms the silver and 39.76, not the running order. These facts are preserved with their separate evidentiary strengths.

Verification rule: A roster is evidence of selection, not proof of playing time; a team result is not an individual statistic.
