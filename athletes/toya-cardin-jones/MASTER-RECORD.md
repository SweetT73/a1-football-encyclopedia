## New original-results discovery — 1995 National Scholastic Outdoor Championships

- **June 16–17, 1995 — Fifth Annual National Scholastic Track and Field Championships**, North Carolina State University, Raleigh, North Carolina: **Toya Jones, Refugio, Texas, placed THIRD in the men's long jump at 23 ft 10¾ in (23-10.75)**, with recorded wind **+0.6 m/s**. The original published meet-results listing names him explicitly. The winner was **Jobe Hannon**, Charlton County, Folkston, Georgia, **24 ft 7 in**, followed by **Ernest Bowser**, Uniondale, New York, **24 ft ¼ in**. [1995 meet results, original results reproduced by DyeStat/RunnerSpace, published July 6, 1995, men's long jump](https://www.dyestat.com/eprofile.php?do=news&event_id=14188&news_id=616830).
- **Event distinction:** This was a national scholastic invitational, **not** the Texas UIL state championship meet, the Texas Relays, or an NCAA event. Do not add this bronze-equivalent third-place finish to his established **17 UIL state-meet medals**; it is a separate competition and the results do not specify an awarded medal.
- **Source limits:** The published meet summary establishes final placement, mark, wind, school and venue, but does **not** provide Jones's individual attempt-by-attempt series or a specific long-jump competition date within June 16–17. Do not invent either.

## November 14, 1998 — Texas A&M vs Missouri, individually documented special-teams play

- **Date and opponent:** Saturday **November 14, 1998**, Texas A&M defeated Missouri **17–14** at Kyle Field, College Station. The **Texas A&M Athletics original game retrospective** identifies **senior safety and special-teams player Toya Jones** as the man who **recovered a muffed Missouri punt at the Missouri 30-yard line with 5:26 remaining**. This recovery set up Russell Bynum's eventual game-winning **39-yard field goal** with **1:30 left**. [Texas A&M Athletics, contemporaneous 1998 game account](https://12thman.com/news/1998/11/14/6-texas-am-17-13-missouri-14); [University of Missouri's archived Associated Press report, November 14, 1998](https://mutigers.com/news/1999/06/21/football-falls-to-texas-am).
- **Scope:** This is one **individually documented 1998 collegiate special-teams fumble recovery**. Do not count it as a high-school 1991–94 statistic, an interception, a forced fumble, or an additional recovery beyond previously established 1998 season totals. The source does not identify Jones as the player who caused Missouri's muff.
- **Prior A1 research:** The same play was described in the October 26 A1 Library research board. Its original Texas A&M game report was directly inspected in this consolidation; no new image was supplied by the source.

## December 5, 1998 — Big 12 Championship, final-play tackle and source disagreement

- **Original Texas A&M Athletics game retrospective:** In the Big 12 Championship Game against Kansas State on **December 5, 1998**, Kansas State quarterback Michael Bishop's last-play regulation Hail Mary was caught by **Everett Burnett at the Texas A&M 1-yard line**, and **Toya Jones drove Burnett backward to prevent a touchdown**. The game went to overtime; Texas A&M won **36–33 in two overtimes**. [Texas A&M Athletics, December 5, 1998 game report](https://12thman.com/news/1998/12/05/10-texas-am-36-1-kansas-state-33).
- **Contemporary description discrepancy:** The *Washington Post*'s **December 6, 1998** report describes Burnett as tackled by Jones at the **3-yard line**, rather than the **1-yard line** in Texas A&M's account. Preserve this difference in the **reported tackle spot**; both sources attribute the touchdown-saving tackle to Jones. [Washington Post, December 6, 1998](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/sports/1998/12/06/ucla-kansas-st-bowled-over/b1ca29f1-bf4b-430d-b9a4-454c158cab70/).
- **Statistical safeguard:** This is a documented individual tackle on the last play of **regulation**, not a game-winning tackle in overtime, a pass breakup, an interception, or an additional game. The reported spot should not be silently normalized to a single yard line until original play-by-play or film resolves it.

## July 11, 1995 — original Battalion interview and 1995 national-meet reconciliation

- **Original contemporary article:** *The Battalion* (Texas A&M), **Tuesday, July 11, 1995, p. 4**, “Aggie freshman ready to prove himself all over again,” interviews **Toya Jones** after his final Refugio high-school track season. It reports a **strained left hamstring at the May 1995 UIL state meet** and a **strained right hamstring at the June USA Junior meet**, where he was limited to **two long-jump attempts** and **withdrew from the 100 meters**. These are the newspaper's reported injury effects, not independently verified medical records. [Original Texas A&M newspaper page](https://newspaper.library.tamu.edu/lccn/sn86088544/1995-07-11/ed-1/seq-4/ocr/).
- **June 10, 1995 — Golden West Invitational, Sacramento:** The article reports **fifth in the long jump and sixth in the 100 meters**. It gives no individual marks; do not invent them or treat these placements as UIL medals.
- **June 17, 1995 — Raleigh, North Carolina:** The article calls his **third-place long-jump** result the **“Golden South Championships.”** The independently preserved results of the **National Scholastic Outdoor Championships**, also held **June 16–17 in Raleigh**, list Jones **third in the long jump at 23 ft 10¾ in**. Because the city, dates, athlete, event and placing overlap, these **may be two descriptions of the same competition**. **Do not count them as two separate third-place results** without event-program confirmation. Preserve both source titles and the uncertainty.
- **June 1995 USA Junior Championships:** *The Battalion* identifies **June 23** and **seventh place in the long jump**, with the right-hamstring issue above. An earlier A1 result cites a different meet date and a **7.18 m** mark. Preserve the discrepancy pending the original championship result sheet; do not invent a verified final mark from this newspaper.
- **1995 UIL state-meet accounting:** The article independently describes **five 1995 medals** (two gold, two silver, one bronze) and **17 career state-meet medals**. These confirm the preexisting total and **must not be added again** as new medals.
