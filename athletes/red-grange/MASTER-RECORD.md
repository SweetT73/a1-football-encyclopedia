# Harold Edward “Red” Grange — A1 permanent individual record

**Identity:** Harold Edward Grange (June 13, 1903 – January 28, 1991), known as “Red Grange,” “The Galloping Ghost,” and “The Wheaton Iceman.” University of Illinois halfback, later professional football player. The existing A1 research document identifies No. 77 and his two-way-era defensive work.

## Historical career chronology — existing A1 research

- **1923–1925, Illinois:** College football halfback. The A1 working profile attributes four touchdowns in the first 12 minutes against Michigan on October 18, 1924, with scoring runs of 95, 67, 56, and 44 yards, and 402 rushing yards on 21 carries to the National Football Foundation. Check the original NFF entry before using the numbers in a published statistics table.
- **1925, Chicago Bears (NFL):** Professional debut on Thanksgiving, November 26, 1925, in a 0–0 tie with the Chicago Cardinals; the subsequent Bears barnstorming tour helped publicize the young NFL.
- **1926, New York Yankees (American Football League):** This football club must not be confused with the baseball Yankees.
- **1927, New York Yankees (NFL):** The franchise entered the NFL; Grange suffered a serious knee injury against Chicago.
- **1928:** The existing A1 research lists no playing season because of the knee injury.
- **1929–1934, Chicago Bears (NFL):** Returned as a two-way player; the A1 working profile describes a late defensive tackle in the 1933 championship game. The Bears won NFL titles in 1932 and 1933 during this period.
- **Hall of Fame:** College Football Hall of Fame, 1951; Pro Football Hall of Fame charter class, 1963, according to the existing A1 research.
