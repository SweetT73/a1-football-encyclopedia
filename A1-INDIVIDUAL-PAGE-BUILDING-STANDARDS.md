# A1 Individual Page-Building Standards — permanent instructions

**Scope:** Every individual biography/life-history page within **A1 All In 1 — Comprehensive History of American Football**. This is an encyclopedia-wide visual and structural standard, **not** a separate Toya photo archive or finished Toya page.

## Approved example — exact image supplied by Sweet T

The exact user-supplied screenshot **12469.png** (November 3, 2026) is the *approved layout and stylistic reference*. Its original bytes are filed as **A1_Individual_Page_Visual_Reference_12469.png** in the existing A1 Google Drive project root, file ID `18uLt-Frp7wlZqfGpUrFHx9t0aqy8dwn3`.

**This example establishes only the visual design and organizational presentation.** Do **not** replicate its inaccurate identities, fabricated photos, unsupported teams, faulty dates, invented statistics, or short/incomplete life story.

## Page presentation

- Create a richly detailed, dramatic, polished **scrollable biography page**. Use the reference's broad navy/black, orange/copper and cream color story; compelling large header/portrait composition; integrated authentic photographs; distinct chronology strips; readable information blocks/cards; elegant visual continuity.
- The reference page is **much too short** and does **not** contain enough material. There is **no arbitrary height/page limit**. A complete individual entry might require **twenty or more screen-length scroll sections**, depending on how much truthful source material exists. Never cut history merely to make it resemble the screenshot's limited length.
- Build from the person's **whole life**, not just athletic statistics. Divide sections clearly by actual periods and context, maintaining chronological order and giving each important period enough space.
- Core life periods to include when applicable: **childhood/early life → high-school years (school, football, other athletics) → college years (football and other athletics) → documented professional athletic career → professional working life after athletics, family, friendships, community and other accomplishments → later life → death, remembrance and legacy**. Do not imply a stage occurred if evidence does not establish it. A living person's page naturally has no death section.
- If a period requires multiple scroll screens, continue that section rather than compressing its history. If an individual has more sports or a nontraditional career, tailor sections to the real facts while preserving the life chronology.
- **Using the same authentic photograph more than once is allowed** when it serves distinct, relevant content or a different place within the same period. Reuse with design purpose; no artificial ban on repetition. Never replace authentic material with invented images just to create visual variety.
- Photographs should complement each relevant passage or time period. An exact era may have limited imagery; retain factual accuracy and reuse authentic photos if useful rather than inventing an unrecorded moment.
- **Strict period-specific image rule:** Within any chronological life-stage section, include **only authentic photographs depicting the subject during that same life period**. For example, the high-school section must not show college, professional, or later-adulthood photographs; the college section must not show photographs from other eras. Reusing a genuine photograph in multiple places **within its correct period** is allowed, but not as a substitute for a photograph from a different period.
- **Full-life montage exception:** A prominent introductory, surrounding, or top-to-bottom life-spanning montage may combine genuine photographs from different periods to visually represent the subject's whole life. Such a montage is separate from any particular period's documentary photo grouping; it does not justify inserting an out-of-period photograph into a dated section.
- **Unknown dates:** If a photograph's era is uncertain, do not treat a guessed date as established. Use the user's supplied working classification where provided, preserving any relevant uncertainty; otherwise keep it in a general/full-life presentation until its period can be placed accurately.
- Use readable headings, periods, dates, captions and citations as appropriate to an encyclopedia page. Do not confuse this *webpage* standard with the separate **text-free standalone photographic collage** instructions for Toya.
- Verify information/visual identity. The screenshot is an aesthetic template, **not a factual source** for Toya or anybody else. Do not use its invented sport/team imagery to document a real career.
- Preserve existing published page material and working links when revising; add evidence-based information instead of silently replacing approved histories.

## Permanent filing and reuse

- The A1 repository is the encyclopedia site. Each person's **one canonical A1 life-history folder** contains their full lifetime materials, originals, provenance and deliverables; useful subfolders are allowed **inside** that person's folder. Do not create duplicate primary folders or temporary working/checkpoint folders.
- This one permanent **A1-wide standard** applies to each individual's page, not only Toya. Store the example at the A1 project level as a design reference, not a biographical photograph in Toya's folder.
- For any future encyclopedia project (for instance track-and-field), the existing A1 historical material can be reused as the source rather than building a competing master biography.

**Sweet T's controlling distinction:** The reference image shows the *right layout*, but the *wrong images* and insufficient material. Keep the style; rebuild content from the person's actual history and approved images, without fabricating scenes.


## Design correction — November 3, 2026: preserve the original visual architecture

Sweet T compared four screenshots of a later Toya A1 page with the *original* approved portrait-led orange/blue sports infographic (the saved 12469.png reference). The four later screenshots **failed to follow the original design**, even though they reused its colors.

**Do not repeat this failure on Toya or any A1 individual page:**
- Reject page structures that repeatedly show **two equal rectangular photos, a giant dark paragraph, and four/six enormous matching cream boxes**, especially on every subsequent chapter. That is a rigid informational grid, not the illustrated biography Sweet T approved.
- The reference is a **bold, continuous, layered sports-biography composition**: large varied-scale authentic portraits, cropped/cutout overlapping figures where source images permit, layered historical imagery, integrated captions and contextual statistics, and compact year-based records woven around photographs. The designs of adjacent sections should vary according to available material.
- Use dark/navy/orange creatively as photographic background, contrast and section identity rather than a flat empty black field or repetitive dark text blocks. Match the reference's energy, density, typography hierarchy and cohesive horizontal bands.
- Compact statistical records into meaningful chronological groups; reserve large visual weight for important lifetime moments, image portraits, and major distinctions. Many rows of isolated boxes waste space and break the story.
- Do not use visitor-facing biography prose to discuss archive-process topics such as “no photographs were invented,” “approved A1 source,” “archive practice,” or “documentation rule.” Those are internal integrity requirements, not facts about the person's life.
- Artistic freedom applies to **composition, cropping, collage, scale, light, typography, visual arrangement, real-image layering and period-consistent photo reuse**. It does not permit fictional people, historical scenes, teams or false stats.
- The user's authentic photographs are raw visual material: they can recur at suitable places within the correct life era, and can be cropped in different ways for thumbnails, portrait anchors and backgrounds. The visual layout should not be dictated by the number of photo files.
- A life-spanning opening montage can cross eras. All photos **inside a specific dated life-stage section must belong to that period**.
- The original image is a **visual model**, not an accurate historical source and not a finished-length biography. Expand it to the full timeline (potentially twenty or more scrolling screens) without abandoning its cinematic integrated layout.

**Acceptance test:** Seen beside the 12469.png reference, the new page should clearly have the *same graphic design language and layered photographic rhythm*, not merely the same orange/cream/navy palette. If every chapter looks like a repeated database card template, it fails and must be redesigned before presentation.



## Narrative and imagery hierarchy — November 3, 2026

The A1 individual pages are **educational, comprehensive life-history articles**, not memorial photo galleries. The narrative, documentation, statistics, historical context, and entire life take precedence; images **support and animate the story rather than becoming the story**. The visual model still controls styling: dynamic photographic cutouts, layered hero compositions, tightly integrated compact record panels, and varied scrolling page sections—not long chains of mostly photographs or giant repetitive stats-only cards.

Use **only actual user-supplied and approved source images of the relevant person** for depictions of that person's life. Do not use invented sports action, substitute faces, made-up teams, or AI-created historical subjects. Appropriate crop, tonal restoration, colorization of original monochrome material, or monochrome versions of an original color photo are allowed. Multiple crops/versions can be used in the webpage without archiving duplicate source files. Use available historical team groups as atmosphere without inventing individual names or dates. Within a period-specific section, use photographs from that life period; full-life introductory montages may span periods.

Use judgment about age and broad era when images are old or ambiguous, relying on Sweet T's working placement and avoiding invented exact dates or unverified identifications. The finished result is a **real, freely scrollable webpage** in the approved infographic style, not a generated single-image approximation pretending to be a webpage.


## Educational biography, imagery as supporting material

A1 pages teach the subject's entire life and achievements. They are not mourning-focused memorial galleries. The written historical story, verified facts and achievements are primary. Actual photographs keep the account lively but are supporting illustrations, not the centerpiece of every section. Reuse, crop, colorize or convert genuine pictures to monochrome as needed while preserving the originals. An undated image may be used within the broad appropriate life period; never fabricate identities or exact dates. The approved visual model remains a continuous, dramatic, scrolling sports-encyclopedia page, not a static poster or repeated photo-heavy blocks.
