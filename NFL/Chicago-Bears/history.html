Origins: Decatur, Illinois (1919)

The Bears trace their roots to the football club sponsored by the A. E. Staley Manufacturing Company of Decatur, Illinois. A contemporary report in the Decatur Daily Review, published Sunday, September 21, 1919, described a meeting of the plant's Welfare Society on the previous Saturday morning, September 20, 1919. Attendees decided to put a first-class football team on the field, and Fritz Wasem, a former Millikin athlete, was selected as manager.

Historical precision: September 20 is a documented organizational meeting date, not a universally designated official franchise birthday. The club's own history and the Staley Museum both date the team's origins to 1919. This 1919 organization preceded George Halas's 1920 role with the team.

Primary newspaper transcription: Decatur Daily Review, September 21, 1919. Institutional account: Staley Museum.

League founding and the Decatur Staleys (1920)

George Halas joined the organization as a player and coach in 1920. On September 17, 1920, Halas represented the Decatur Staleys at the Canton, Ohio meeting that established the American Professional Football Association (APFA), renamed the National Football League in 1922. The Staleys were a founding member, entering the league one year before the Green Bay Packers joined in 1921.

Sources: Pro Football Hall of Fame and Chicago Bears official historical highlights.

Move to Chicago and new name (1921–1922)

In 1921, A. E. Staley transferred the club to Halas, contributed $5,000 toward its relocation, and requested that the team retain the Staleys name for the season. The Chicago Staleys won the 1921 APFA championship. On January 28, 1922, the franchise was renamed the Chicago Bears.

The name drew on the club's association with the Chicago Cubs' ballpark: Halas reasoned that football players were larger than baseball players, so Bears would be a fitting counterpart to Cubs.

Franchise colors and visual identity

The Chicago Bears are associated with dark navy blue, burnt orange, and white. Their modern identity developed over time; the original Decatur Staleys uniforms were not the same as today's Bears uniforms. This encyclopedia page uses navy, orange, and white—not gold.

Source: Chicago Bears uniform history, official media guide.

Championship legacy

The Bears have won nine NFL championships: eight in the pre-Super Bowl era and one Super Bowl championship. The franchise's landmark Super Bowl victory came in Super Bowl XX, following the 1985 season, under head coach Mike Ditka.

The Bears later reached Super Bowl XLI following the 2006 season under head coach Lovie Smith. Smith also guided Chicago to the NFC Championship Game following the 2010 season, giving him two NFC title-game appearances as Bears head coach (2006 and 2010 seasons).
