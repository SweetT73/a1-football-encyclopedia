## A1 platform and fantasy-football product direction

The A1 encyclopedias are intended to form one connected discovery experience across American football, other sports, films and future subjects, with subject-specific access rights rather than duplicated biographies or incompatible collections. These are product-design directions, not evidence of a deployed subscription platform. The existing permanent [film, streaming and founding-membership master](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1G15aSRD6Itb0AdzadQoXfnll7imy97vywexfzsxpGBM/edit) holds the fuller mobile-first, accessibility, universal-search, membership and film UX specification; preserve it as the controlling topical master instead of copying its full text here.

**Native fantasy football — Sweet T's proposed feature, not an operating service.** Provide a possible free core game with optional premium convenience features. One proposed premium capability is lineup-change reminders and an **explicitly user-authorized** substitution of an eligible bench player when a starter is declared inactive shortly before kickoff. Sweet T's example is a quarterback declared inactive ten minutes before kickoff while the fantasy manager is attending Sunday worship; the aim is to avoid constant phone attention. Actual action must obey the specific league's eligibility, lineup locks, substitution policy and user authorization. No provider integration, data license, commercial tier, pricing or working automated substitution has been established. Keep fantasy-game access separate from paid encyclopedia entitlements.

**Commercial concepts remain proposals.** Individual football, film and other subject subscriptions, combined packages and all-access tiers were discussed; example prices below $5 per subject, around $5 per category and around $10 combined were exploratory, not approved charges. Seasonal peaks in NFL interest and holiday film interest are opportunities for focused offerings, not reasons to discard those collections. Visitor-facing design should support casual questions as well as deep historical research, with readable mobile-first presentation and accessibility for older users. Source: Sweet T's earlier A1 conversation decisions preserved in the existing [conversation audit](research/conversation-audit-2026-11-16.md), sections 2–3 and the Drive-master reconciliation; this section is a permanent project-level crosslink, not a temporary audit record.

## Published A1 pages versus research files — November 19, 2026 direction

Sweet T expressly requested to **see the A1 NFL rushing tracker on its actual A1 webpage before discussing its build or billing**. He rejected a Word document as a substitute for the live page, and objected to placeholders being presented as finished work. This is a **user product and delivery requirement**, not proof that the site is deployed, that visitor traffic exists, or that a billing system operates. Preserve the distinction between a GitHub source page, a publicly reachable deployed page, a functioning automated NFL.com change monitor, and measured visitor traffic; each requires separate direct evidence. Do not substitute mockups, placeholder rankings, or a prose status report for the requested functioning page. Keep NFL.com as the sole authority for current rushing rankings and totals, and do not infer traffic statistics from repository activity. The existing permanent [NFL rushing page](Leagues/NFL/rushing-top-100/index.html) remains the implementation location; this project-level note records the user-facing product requirement without creating a second tracker.
