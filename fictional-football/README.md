# A1 — Fictional American Football

A permanent A1 branch **parallel to real football history**, not a replacement for or subdivision of the actual NFL/college/professional histories.

## Canonical structure
- `teams/`: one permanent record for each fictional football franchise, with canonical roster and personnel links.
- `players/`: one canonical record per fictional athlete, linked to all teams, works and performances.
- `works/`: indexes of fictional football stories by original medium: theatrical films, television films, series/miniseries, books, plays, comics, and other media. Original release venue is separate from later streaming availability.
- `competitions/`: fictional leagues, schedules, seasons, opponents and standings; fictional results clearly distinguished from real competition.
- `evidence/`: scene stills, transcript findings, unresolved jersey numbers and cast identification.
- `visual-canon/`: verified performer/character likeness, period costume, jersey, helmet/equipment and appearance notes; never change identity for illustration.

The film, theatrical, literary, and television catalog keeps its own permanent index; cross-link the SAME team and player records rather than duplicating them. Real performers who played actual pro football link to their real A1 athlete files.

## Evidence discipline
Verified source, film dialogue/character claim, strong inference, and unknown are distinct. Film's real NFL setting does not make its fictional team's statistics historical NFL facts. Do not invent expansion status, displaced team, name, number, actor, measurements, stats, or franchise record. All fiction-context game results and career statistics must be marked in-universe.

## Approved visitor-facing hierarchy (Sweet T clarification, 2026-11-16)
Navigate **A1 > Fictional American Football > Films > Football Films > individual film (e.g., Any Given Sunday or Gus) > fictional teams featured in that film > notable named players, coaches and personnel for each team > full character record**. Parallel branches for television, books, plays and other works are based on the work's original medium. Each film's team list is an index/view into its canonical team record, and each team's player list is an index/view into each canonical character profile. Do not bury all fictional people in one undifferentiated folder; do not create duplicate, divergent biographies or teams. Every character's canonical profile must backlink to team(s), work(s), cast performer(s), film scene and source evidence, and real NFL biography if the performer is also an actual player. Unidentified extras stay in a verified research queue, separate from named players.

**Examples**
- Film: *Gus* (1976) > California Atoms > named players, coach, owner; Michigan Mammoths > known characters.
- Film: *Any Given Sunday* (1999) > Miami Sharks > Willie “Steamin” Beamen and other film-verifiable players; add additional fictional teams as independently documented.

This is the **navigation and filing view**. Underlying GitHub canonical storage may use normalized /teams and /players locations with direct references; pages must expose the work > team > player structure immediately.

## Comics, adaptations, and rights boundaries

Comics belong in the `works/` original-medium index when they depict fictional American football. A seasonal Christmas comic based on a non-football literary work belongs in the separate existing A1 Christmas Comics permanent plan, **not** in this football branch merely because both projects use illustrated storytelling. Cross-link only when a work genuinely includes a football character, team, or scene.

Sweet T's creative direction allows a **deliberate comic-book-style transformation** as a distinct presentation choice; routine cleanup, recoloring, decolorizing, or zooming of a real person's reference must preserve that person's underlying identity and appearance. This is a user design instruction, not a claim that particular generated images have been visually reviewed or cleared for distribution.

Before publishing or selling adaptations, distinguish a public-domain underlying story from separately protected translations, illustrations, character designs, photographs, films, recorded performances, and modern editions. Do not treat a public-domain story as a blanket license to redraw protected film scenes. Film or trailer excerpts for genuine review/commentary require case-specific fair-use analysis; neither fair use nor a later studio license is assumed. These are publication safeguards, not evidence of obtained rights.

## Real football players who appear on screen — identity crosslinks

Preserve **one real-world athlete identity** across competitive football records and acting credits, while keeping any **fictional on-screen character** as a separate character identity linked to that performer. An actor's football training camp, a role in a sports drama, and an authenticated regular-season appearance are different evidence categories. An acting role cannot supply an NFL game played, team season, or fictional character's actual athletic statistics.

The designated A1 Historical Football and Acting Profile collection already has permanent individual documents for [Lee Reherman](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1GVH8FMVzPE16rRcQNVODBWR9U6UZdIt_1ZmCB-KmthA/edit), [Michael Strahan](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1i1aE2iLV-CeGhmUTKxflHzYQYVUQ1-24mLiCuHBMZFs/edit), [Sammy Baugh](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1imlLUxT63H0rEQdwAjpYNguil2PfL-4m3CD06BtYxhA/edit), and [Owen “Boogie” Roberts](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1z3M35LNCVXS5-KPmKAv75HpBLpSDBpveZXTNYjwib2Y/edit). These are **existing records to crosslink**, not invitations to create duplicate biographies. Their previously reviewed source wording establishes these distinctions:

- Reherman's Miami Dolphins training-camp association must **not** be promoted into a regular-season NFL game; American Gladiators' Hawk is an entertainment crosslink.
- Strahan's New York Giants football history and *Magic Mike XXL* screen work belong to one real person's record, with distinct credit types.
