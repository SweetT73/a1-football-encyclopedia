## A1 film and screen-work classification — permanent catalog rules

Sweet T's approved A1 classification separates a work's **original intended release medium** from its **current streaming platform**. Do not reclassify a theatrical film as a streaming original simply because it is available on Netflix or another service today. Preserve these distinct catalog types rather than collapsing all moving-image titles into “movies” or “shows”:

- **Feature films:** original theatrical motion pictures (big screen); made-for-television movies (small screen); direct-to-streaming original feature films; standalone documentary films classified by original release venue; animated feature films.
- **Short films:** live-action shorts, animated shorts, and documentary shorts as separate types.
- **Series:** traditional event miniseries (for example *Roots*, *Salem's Lot*, *Lonesome Dove*, and *Chiefs*); modern limited series with a single planned story; ongoing or continuing television series; and docuseries. An episode count alone does not establish a work's original category.

This taxonomy governs football-related films and their crosslinks to the broader A1 film catalog. Fictional football players, teams and on-screen performances remain canonical in [A1 Fictional American Football](../fictional-football/README.md), while real athletes remain in their permanent athlete profiles. A film entry may link to both without duplicating either identity.

### A1 independent film selections and review-video direction

Sweet T proposed annual independent favorite-film selections or awards, potentially opening nominations around the Academy's Oscar nomination announcement, reviewing eligible nominated films before the ceremony, and announcing A1 selections near the ceremony. **This is a proposed editorial calendar**, not a launched award or a fixed 2027 commitment. His intended starting approach for video criticism is genuine review/commentary with selected relevant film or trailer excerpts evaluated for fair use **case by case**. Neither copyright clearance nor an automatic fair-use guarantee follows from calling a video a review; studio publicity access or licensing can be considered later. Preserve creator credits, source references and the difference between footage of actual football competition and fictional dramatization.
